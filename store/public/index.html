      About this demonstration store

      Demo Store is a working demonstration of a small online shop, built for one client and shown here so the design and behaviour can be reviewed in a real browser. It is not a live shop and it is not accepting customers.

      The catalogue, prices, stock levels and shipping options you see are real fields read from the store's own database, so the behaviour shown is the behaviour the finished store would have. Payment is deliberately not connected: the store is wired to a sandbox/test payment account only, and test card 4242 4242 4242 4242 (any future date, any 3-digit code) walks through the checkout.

      Nothing is charged, no card details are entered on this site, no orders are placed, and no goods ship. No cookies are set and no third-party scripts or resources load on any page.

      How payment methods work

      Card payments work immediately, for everyone, everywhere. Everything else takes longer to set up, and some of it changes how a store actually operates — so I start every store with card only, and add others when there is a reason to.

      Card. Nothing to configure. Every customer, every country, no extra requirements.

      Apple Pay and Google Pay. Usually a single switch. Apple Pay also needs a small verification file hosted on the store's own domain.

      Klarna, Affirm, and Afterpay. These need business verification, a minimum order value, and supported countries. They also change who carries the risk when a customer disputes a charge. That is not a reason to avoid them, but it is a business decision rather than a setting.

      Bank transfers — ACH, SEPA, and similar. Business verification again, and they settle in days rather than seconds. The money arrives later, which changes a store's cash flow.

      Cash App and Amazon Pay. Available in specific countries only.

      Turning any of them on is one switch. Turning them on well is the part worth thinking about. I will tell you which ones suit your store, which ones I would leave off, and why.

      About the product images

      This is a demonstration store, so the products are not real and there is nothing to photograph.

      The shapes you see stand in for the three kinds of thing a store can sell — physical goods, digital downloads, and services. When I build a real store, the owner supplies their own photographs. I set up the place for them, and they decide what goes in it.

      I do not use stock photographs of real products on a demonstration page. A photo of something that is not for sale, on a page that looks like a shop, is a misunderstanding waiting to happen.
